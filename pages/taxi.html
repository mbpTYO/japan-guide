How to Take a Taxi

タクシーの乗り方

← All articles / 記事一覧へ

Japanese taxis are clean, safe and easy to use once you know the routine. The meter works out the fare — drivers do not bargain and do not expect a tip — and if you do not speak Japanese, you can call a taxi with an app and enter your destination on the map. This guide covers how to find one, what happens in the car, what the meter means, and the extra fees the apps add.

日本のタクシーは清潔で安全で、手順を知っていれば簡単に使えます。運賃はメーターで計算され、値交渉やチップは不要です。日本語が話せなくても、アプリで行き先を指定して呼ぶことができます。ここでは、探し方・乗車中のマナー・運賃の仕組み・アプリの追加料金をまとめます。

1. Find a taxi タクシーを探す

There are three normal ways to get one. In big cities all three work; in rural areas there are fewer cruising taxis, but you will usually find one at the station.

  1. At a taxi stand (タクシー乗り場, takushī noriba): look outside stations, airports, large hotels and department stores. Join the queue — the first person in line takes the first cab.
  2. On the street: raise your hand where it is safe for the car to stop. Check the panel in the front windshield first: red means available (it usually says 空車, kūsha, "vacant"), green means occupied. A light on the roof can also show availability at night. A sign reading 迎車 (geisha) means the taxi is on its way to collect someone else.
  3. By app, phone or through your hotel: useful late at night or off the beaten track. If you call a taxi, a pick-up fee (迎車料金) is usually added.

駅・空港・ホテルなどの「タクシー乗り場」で並ぶか、安全な場所で空車を見つけて手を上げます。フロントガラスの表示は赤が空車、緑が使用中。アプリや電話で呼ぶと迎車料金がかかる場合があります。

2. Get in and tell the driver where to go 乗車と目的地の伝え方

  • Do not touch the door. The rear door on the kerb side opens and closes automatically — the driver operates it. Wait for it to open, both when getting in and getting out. If you have luggage, the driver will open the boot; just ask.
  • Wear a seat belt in every seat, including the back. It is required on all roads in Japan.
  • Show the destination in writing. Many drivers, especially outside the big cities, do not speak English. Show the address or hotel name in Japanese, or a pin on your phone. Say 「〜までお願いします」 (... made onegaishimasu, "to ..., please"). Taxis in Tokyo carry a phrase sheet in English, Korean and Chinese that you can point to.
  • Six or more people? A standard taxi takes up to four adults, so book two cars for a larger group. Ask in advance if you have several large suitcases.
  • Expressway or not? On longer trips the driver may ask if you want to use the toll road. It is usually faster, but you pay the toll.

3. What the fare is made of 運賃の仕組み

Fares are set region by region and are almost always calculated by the meter. The example below is for a standard taxi in Tokyo's 23 wards, Musashino and Mitaka, with rates that took effect on 20 April 2026. Other cities set their own rates.

ItemCharge
Initial fare (first 1.0 km)¥500

These are maximum approved rates; a few companies charge slightly less (for example, a ¥480 start). The same route can cost a little more or less depending on traffic, because time is charged as well as distance. As a rough guide, JNTO puts a 7 km Tokyo ride of about 25 minutes at ¥2,500–3,000 in daytime. Airport routes often have a fixed flat fare instead of the meter — ask before you set off, and check the price in advance.

運賃は地域ごとに決まり、基本的にメーターで計算します。上の表は2026年4月20日から適用された東京23区・武蔵野市・三鷹市の普通車の例です。初乗り1.0kmまで500円、以降232mごとに100円。低速時は時間でも加算され、22時から翌5時は2割増。高速料金は乗客負担です。空港などには定額運賃もあるので乗車前に確認しましょう。

4. Calling a taxi with an app 配車アプリを使う

Apps are the easiest option if you are shy of speaking Japanese: the destination is already set on the map. JNTO lists S.RIDE, Uber, DiDi and GO as having an English service. In Japan these apps mostly call licensed taxis — even when the app is one you know from home, do not be surprised if an ordinary taxi arrives.

5. Pay and get out 支払いと下車

  • Pay the meter amount plus any tolls when you arrive. Most city taxis accept credit cards, and many accept IC cards such as Suica and PASMO, Apple Pay/Google Pay and QR payment. Stickers on the door or window show what is accepted — check them before you set off, and carry some yen because rural taxis may be cash only.
  • Do not tip. Pay what the meter says; a simple "arigatō gozaimasu" is enough.
  • Ask for the receipt (領収書, ryōshūsho) if you might need it. It shows which company you used, which is the fastest way to trace a phone or bag left in the car.
  • Wait for the door to open for you, then get out on the kerb side.

Watch out for 注意点

  • Use licensed taxis only. Licensed cabs have green number plates and wait at proper taxi stands. Japan's transport ministry warns against unlicensed "shirotaku" cars and against hire-car drivers who solicit passengers at airports: hire cars are reservation-only. Politely refuse anyone who approaches you at the airport offering a ride — pre-book at the official counter instead.
  • Ginza at night. In Ginza, Tokyo, taxis may only pick up at designated stands on weekdays from 22:00 to 01:00 (excluding weekends and public holidays). Look for the stand.
  • Late-night queues. After the trains stop, especially on Friday and Saturday nights, taxis are scarce and stands can have long lines. Ordering ahead with an app helps.
  • Do not pay an extortionate "flat fare". Fixed airport fares are official and published; agree the price before getting in if the driver proposes one.

Quick checklist 確認リスト

  • Install and set up a taxi app before you travel, or save your address in Japanese.
  • Look for red / 空車, or join the queue at a taxi stand.
  • Wait for the automatic door, then buckle up in the back.
  • Show the destination in Japanese or as a map pin.
  • Check the payment stickers and carry some cash.
  • Pay the meter amount, skip the tip, and keep the receipt.
